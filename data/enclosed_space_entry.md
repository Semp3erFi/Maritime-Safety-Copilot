# Enclosed Space Entry Safety

## Overview

Enclosed spaces aboard ships can contain dangerous atmospheres even when no hazard is immediately visible. Possible conditions include oxygen deficiency, oxygen enrichment, toxic gases or vapours, and flammable atmospheres.

Examples may include tanks, cofferdams, void spaces, ballast tanks, cargo spaces, chain lockers, pump rooms and other spaces with restricted access or inadequate natural ventilation.

An enclosed space should be treated as potentially hazardous until an appropriate assessment demonstrates that entry can be carried out safely.

## Before entry

Entry should be planned and controlled.

Typical precautions include:

- identifying the hazards associated with the space;
- completing an appropriate risk assessment;
- isolating relevant pipelines, machinery or other energy sources where required;
- ventilating the space sufficiently;
- testing the atmosphere with suitable equipment;
- establishing communication arrangements;
- ensuring rescue arrangements are prepared;
- controlling access to the space;
- briefing personnel involved in the operation;
- authorizing entry through the ship's established procedure.

An entry permit is commonly used to confirm that required precautions have been completed before personnel enter.

## Atmosphere testing

Atmospheric conditions should be evaluated before entry and, where necessary, monitored during occupancy.

Testing may need to consider:

- oxygen concentration;
- flammable gases or vapours;
- hydrogen sulphide;
- carbon monoxide;
- other toxic substances associated with the space, cargo or previous contents.

A safe reading at one location does not automatically mean that the entire space is safe. Different gases may accumulate at different levels and conditions can change while work is being performed.

Ventilation should therefore be maintained when required by the risk assessment and entry procedure.

## During entry

An attendant should remain outside when required by the applicable procedure and maintain effective communication with personnel inside.

Conditions should be reassessed if circumstances change. Entry should be stopped when safe conditions can no longer be confirmed.

Personnel should not introduce additional hazards into the space without considering them in the risk assessment. Examples include hot work, chemicals, painting operations or equipment that may affect the atmosphere.

## Rescue

An enclosed-space casualty may tempt another crew member to enter immediately. Unplanned rescue attempts can create additional casualties.

Rescue arrangements should therefore be prepared before entry begins. Personnel involved in rescue should be trained, suitable equipment should be available, and entry for rescue should follow the applicable emergency procedure.

## Regulatory context

IMO adopted revised recommendations for entering enclosed spaces aboard ships in Resolution MSC.581(110) in 2025.

SOLAS also addresses atmosphere-testing equipment for enclosed spaces and enclosed-space entry and rescue drills.

## Important note

This file is an educational summary. Actual entry must follow the ship's SMS, current statutory requirements, risk assessment, permit system and applicable company procedures.
