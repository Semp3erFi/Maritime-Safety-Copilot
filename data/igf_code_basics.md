# IGF Code Basics

## Overview

The International Code of Safety for Ships Using Gases or Other Low-flashpoint Fuels (IGF Code) provides an international safety framework for ships using fuels that introduce hazards different from conventional marine fuel oils.

The Code applies to relevant ships using gas or other low-flashpoint fuels and addresses safety aspects associated with the arrangement, installation, control and operation of fuel systems.

## Safety philosophy

The objective is to achieve a high level of safety, reliability and dependability while considering the specific characteristics of the fuel being used.

Fuel characteristics may introduce hazards such as:

- fire;
- explosion;
- gas release;
- cryogenic exposure;
- pressure hazards;
- toxicity for certain fuels;
- loss of containment;
- hazardous atmospheres.

The exact hazards depend on the fuel and system design.

## Areas addressed

Fuel-system safety may involve:

- fuel containment;
- piping systems;
- bunkering arrangements;
- ventilation;
- gas or vapour detection;
- fire protection;
- electrical equipment;
- control and monitoring systems;
- emergency shutdown arrangements;
- machinery-space configuration;
- operational procedures;
- training and competence.

The Code follows a goal-based safety philosophy in which design and operational measures should address the risks created by the fuel.

## Bunkering

Bunkering low-flashpoint fuels requires careful coordination between the receiving ship and supplying facility.

Planning may include:

- communication between responsible personnel;
- verification of connections;
- control of ignition sources;
- establishment of appropriate safety zones;
- monitoring for leakage;
- pressure and temperature control;
- emergency shutdown arrangements;
- procedures for starting, stopping and completing transfer;
- emergency-response readiness.

The exact requirements depend on the fuel, ship design and applicable regulations.

## Detection and ventilation

A release of gaseous or volatile fuel can create a hazardous atmosphere.

Gas-detection systems and appropriate ventilation arrangements are therefore important safety barriers in relevant spaces.

Detection should provide sufficiently early warning for personnel or automatic safety systems to take appropriate action.

## Emergency shutdown

Emergency shutdown systems may be used to stop fuel flow or isolate sections of a fuel system when unsafe conditions are detected.

The objective is to limit escalation following a leak, abnormal pressure condition, equipment failure or other hazardous event.

## Training and human factors

Technical safeguards alone are not sufficient.

Personnel involved in operation, maintenance and bunkering should understand:

- fuel properties;
- associated hazards;
- normal procedures;
- abnormal conditions;
- emergency actions;
- relevant protective equipment.

Competence requirements should be considered together with the ship's procedures and applicable STCW requirements.

## Important note

The IGF regulatory framework continues to develop as new marine fuels and technologies are introduced. Applicability can differ between fuels. Current IMO instruments, flag-State requirements, class rules and approved ship-specific documentation must be consulted for actual operations.
