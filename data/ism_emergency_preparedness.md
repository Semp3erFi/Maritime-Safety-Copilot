# ISM Code - Emergency Preparedness

## Overview

The International Safety Management (ISM) Code provides a framework for the safe management and operation of ships and for pollution prevention. It requires shipping companies to establish and maintain a Safety Management System (SMS) appropriate to their ships and operations.

Emergency preparedness is an important part of an effective SMS. A company should identify potential emergency situations that may occur on board and establish procedures for responding to them.

## Emergency preparedness

Emergency planning should consider credible shipboard scenarios such as:

- fire and explosion;
- collision;
- grounding;
- flooding;
- loss of propulsion or steering;
- pollution incidents;
- person overboard;
- enclosed-space emergencies;
- cargo-related emergencies;
- serious injury or medical emergencies;
- loss of electrical power.

Procedures should define what actions are expected, who is responsible for those actions, how communications are established, and how shipboard and shore-based personnel cooperate during an emergency.

## Drills and exercises

Written procedures alone are not sufficient. Personnel should be familiar with their emergency responsibilities and should practise relevant scenarios through drills and exercises.

Drills help verify whether:

- personnel understand their duties;
- alarms and communication systems work as intended;
- emergency equipment is available and usable;
- procedures are practical;
- response times are acceptable;
- weaknesses exist in the emergency plan.

Lessons identified during drills, exercises, incidents or near misses should be considered when reviewing procedures.

## Ship-shore cooperation

Certain emergencies require support from the company ashore. The SMS should therefore establish clear communication arrangements between the ship and shore management.

Shore personnel may assist by coordinating external resources, contacting authorities, communicating with relevant parties and supporting the master and shipboard team.

## Key principle

Emergency preparedness is a continuous management process:

**identify credible emergencies -> establish procedures -> train personnel -> conduct drills -> evaluate performance -> improve the system.**

An effective SMS should enable the crew to respond in an organized manner rather than having to improvise critical actions after an emergency has already developed.
