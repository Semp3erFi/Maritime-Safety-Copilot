# Permit to Work System

## Overview

A Permit to Work (PTW) is a formal control process used for work that presents significant hazards or requires specific safety precautions.

The permit does not make a hazardous activity safe by itself. Its purpose is to ensure that hazards have been considered, necessary controls have been established, responsibilities are clear, and the work has been formally authorized before it begins.

## Typical applications

Depending on the ship and company SMS, permits may be used for activities such as:

- hot work;
- enclosed-space entry;
- electrical work;
- working aloft;
- working over the side;
- maintenance requiring machinery isolation;
- work involving pressure systems;
- other non-routine or high-risk activities.

The exact permit categories are defined by the company's Safety Management System.

## Typical PTW process

A simplified permit process may include:

1. Define the work.
2. Identify associated hazards.
3. Conduct or review the risk assessment.
4. Establish required control measures.
5. Isolate relevant equipment or systems where necessary.
6. Verify that the workplace is ready.
7. Brief the personnel performing the work.
8. Obtain authorization from the responsible person.
9. Perform the work under the stated conditions.
10. Suspend the work if conditions change.
11. Close the permit after confirming the work is complete and the area is safe.

## Permit to Work versus checklist

A checklist and a permit serve related but different purposes.

A **checklist** helps personnel verify that specified actions or conditions have been considered.

A **Permit to Work** forms part of an authorization and control process. It normally identifies the work, location, hazards, controls, validity period, responsible personnel and required approvals.

A checklist may be incorporated into a permit, but completing a checklist does not automatically provide authorization to begin hazardous work.

## Validity

A permit should apply only to the activity, location and conditions for which it was issued.

If important conditions change, the permit may need to be suspended or cancelled and the situation reassessed.

Examples include:

- change in atmospheric conditions;
- change in weather;
- change in personnel;
- change in the scope of work;
- failure of ventilation;
- removal of an isolation;
- simultaneous operations introducing new hazards.

## Communication

Personnel performing the task should understand:

- what work is authorized;
- what hazards are present;
- which control measures must remain in place;
- what conditions require work to stop;
- who is responsible for supervision;
- what emergency arrangements apply.

## Key principle

A permit is not merely paperwork.

An effective PTW system provides a structured link between **risk assessment, authorization, communication, operational control and safe completion of work**.

## Important note

Permit requirements vary between companies, ships, flag States and operations. The ship's approved SMS and applicable procedures must always be followed.
