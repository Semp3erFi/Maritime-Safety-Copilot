# Port State Control Basics

## Overview

Port State Control (PSC) is the inspection of foreign ships visiting a country's ports to verify compliance with applicable international requirements.

The primary responsibility for ensuring that a ship complies with international standards lies with the flag State. PSC provides an additional mechanism for identifying ships that may not meet required standards.

It is therefore often described as a second line of defence against substandard shipping.

## Scope of inspection

A PSC inspection may consider areas such as:

- statutory certificates and documents;
- structural and equipment condition;
- fire safety;
- life-saving appliances;
- pollution-prevention arrangements;
- navigation equipment;
- working and living conditions;
- crew certification;
- operational readiness;
- implementation of safety-management requirements.

The exact scope depends on the type of inspection, applicable conventions, ship characteristics and findings made by the Port State Control Officer.

## Initial inspection

An inspection commonly begins with verification of relevant certificates and an overall assessment of the ship's condition.

The officer may consider whether there are indications that the ship, equipment, crew or safety-management arrangements fail to meet applicable requirements.

## Clear grounds and more detailed inspection

When observations provide reason to believe that the ship or its equipment does not substantially correspond with required standards, or that required procedures are not being effectively implemented, a more detailed inspection may follow.

The purpose is not simply to identify paperwork errors. PSC may examine whether the ship is genuinely capable of operating safely and in compliance with applicable instruments.

## Deficiencies

A deficiency is a condition found during inspection that does not comply with an applicable requirement.

Depending on its seriousness, a deficiency may require:

- correction before departure;
- correction within a specified period;
- correction at another port;
- additional verification;
- detention of the ship.

Not every deficiency results in detention.

## Detention

Detention is a serious control action.

It may be imposed where deficiencies are sufficiently serious that the ship should not be allowed to proceed to sea until the relevant safety, environmental or compliance concerns have been addressed as required.

The decision depends on the nature and seriousness of the deficiencies and the applicable PSC procedures.

## Regional cooperation

Port States cooperate through regional PSC regimes to improve consistency and share inspection information.

Examples include the Paris MoU in Europe and the North Atlantic and the Tokyo MoU in the Asia-Pacific region.

Regional cooperation helps authorities identify higher-risk ships and reduce unnecessary duplication of inspections.

## Safety Management System

PSC can also consider whether the ship's Safety Management System is effectively implemented.

Evidence of significant operational problems, repeated deficiencies, poor maintenance or lack of crew familiarity may indicate weaknesses extending beyond an isolated technical defect.

## Current IMO framework

IMO periodically updates its Procedures for Port State Control. The 2025 procedures were adopted as Resolution A.1206(34).

## Key principle

PSC can be viewed as:

**Flag State responsibility -> primary compliance mechanism**

**Port State Control -> independent additional check when foreign ships enter port**

## Important note

PSC procedures, targeting methods and inspection practices can differ between regional regimes and authorities. Current IMO procedures and the rules of the applicable regional PSC regime should be consulted for operational or compliance decisions.
