# SOLAS Chapter IX - Management for the Safe Operation of Ships

## Overview

SOLAS Chapter IX addresses management for the safe operation of ships and gives mandatory effect to the International Safety Management Code for ships to which the requirements apply.

The chapter connects international statutory requirements with the company's Safety Management System.

## Purpose of the ISM framework

The ISM framework is intended to promote:

- safe ship operation;
- prevention of injury or loss of life;
- prevention of environmental damage, particularly to the marine environment;
- systematic management of operational risks;
- clear responsibilities between shipboard and shore management;
- continual improvement of safety management.

The system is deliberately based on broad management principles because ships and shipping companies operate under different conditions.

## Company responsibilities

A shipping company should establish, implement and maintain an SMS that meets applicable ISM requirements.

The SMS should provide a structured system for managing matters such as:

- safety and environmental protection policy;
- responsibilities and authority;
- master's responsibility and authority;
- resources and personnel;
- shipboard operations;
- emergency preparedness;
- reporting and analysis of non-conformities, accidents and hazardous occurrences;
- maintenance of the ship and equipment;
- documentation;
- internal verification and management review.

## Master

The master has a central role in implementation of the SMS on board.

The company's management system should clearly establish the master's authority and responsibilities and support effective communication between the ship and shore organization.

Safety management should not prevent the master from exercising necessary professional judgment when immediate decisions are required for safety or environmental protection.

## Certification

The ISM system includes company and ship certification.

A **Document of Compliance (DOC)** demonstrates that the company has been assessed against applicable ISM requirements for the relevant ship type or types.

A **Safety Management Certificate (SMC)** demonstrates that the ship operates under the company's approved safety management system.

Certification is supported by audits and verification rather than being based only on possession of written manuals.

## Audits and continual improvement

An SMS should be implemented in practice.

Audits, incident reports, near misses, non-conformities, drills and operational experience provide information that can be used to identify weaknesses and improve the system.

The effectiveness of safety management therefore depends on both documented procedures and the safety culture with which those procedures are applied.

## Key relationship

A useful conceptual relationship is:

**SOLAS Chapter IX -> makes the ISM framework mandatory**

**ISM Code -> defines the safety-management framework**

**Company SMS -> applies that framework to the company's actual ships and operations**
